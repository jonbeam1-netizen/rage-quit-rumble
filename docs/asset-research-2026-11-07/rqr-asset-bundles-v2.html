Licensing and hosting

Unity Asset Store assets can be used in three.js. Unity's EULA never restricts the engine, and their support article says so directly. The condition is that assets ship "embedded" in the product and users can't extract raw files. A browser game can't literally prevent that, so the plan is: pack models into compressed game bundles, never offer downloads, and keep the repo private. This is my reading, not legal advice; a one-line email to Unity support would settle it.

Hosting follows from that. If any Asset Store or Fab purchase goes in, the GitHub repo must be private and the site hosts on Cloudflare Pages or Netlify, free. Friends still just click a link. If you want a public GitHub Pages repo, stick to the Blank Canvas bundle: everything in it is CC0 or CC-BY.

Fab is the friendliest paid store. Its Standard License allows shipping assets inside a project with no file-protection clause. Sketchfab's paid store closed; only its free CC downloads remain. Credit CC-BY authors in a credits screen.

Faces are on us. Agreed that faces get repainted in Blender. That removes the one thing that made most candidates fail, and it is why a clean free body beats a painted paid one here.

Decisions for you

Nothing gets downloaded or built until you say so.

  1. Master body: approve Quaternius Universal Base Characters as the one skeleton everything else is built on? This decision locks the rig for every minigame.
  2. Default look at launch: the free fantasy outfits, repainted, or modern clothes that we paint and model ourselves? Fantasy is ready now; modern is a few days of Blender.
  3. First purchase: Kevin Iglesias Basic Motions at $18, plus the two prop packs at about $11 total? That is the whole Painted Rec Room bundle.
  4. Hosting: OK with a private repo on Cloudflare Pages if paid assets are used?
  5. Skins budget later: the Skins and Trophies bundle is for when achievements exist. I'd start with the free Ser Skav and buy the rest one at a time.
