The brief is now simple: one excellent male and female base, lots of faces and hair, clothes that fit, a standard skeleton with a huge animation ecosystem behind it, and no price cap. Two agents swept character systems and animation libraries; I checked the key claims. Three paths are realistic. One platform that would have been the obvious answer, Ready Player Me, no longer exists.

Three realistic paths

Ratings out of 5: Faces (variety), Hair, Clothes, Web (polycount and export to GLB). Every path uses a Mixamo-compatible or Mixamo-retargetable skeleton, so the same animation stack applies.

The most complete system that exists. Morph-based faces with thousands of sliders, Headshot builds a head from a photo, a deep store of hair and modern casual clothes that already fit the body, a game-ready body of about 11k triangles with automatic LODs, FBX out and GLB through Blender. ActorCore animations play on it with no retargeting and Mixamo clips retarget in one click.

  • Condition 1: the in-game creator must be preset-based: players pick from heads, hair, outfits and colors we export. Face sliders inside the game would need Reallusion's Enterprise license. For a party game, presets are honestly the better design anyway: cheaper to sync across 8 players and no ugly slider faces.
  • Condition 2: Reallusion's EULA (Nov 2026) requires shipped content to be packaged or encrypted, not a plain downloadable GLB. We'd pack meshes into an obfuscated bundle decoded in the page, and I'd email them for a written OK before buying content at scale.

Realistic spend: $299 for CC5, $199 for Headshot if you want faces from photos, then roughly $20 to $60 per hair or outfit pack. Budget $600 to $900 for a strong starter wardrobe.

The MakeHuman successor, living inside Blender. Realistic face and body sliders with no legal limits, so an in-game slider creator is allowed here. Exports GLB directly with a Mixamo-compatible rig. Everything you ship is yours, so a public GitHub repo stays possible.

  • Only 3 official hair packs and 28 clothing packs, mostly dated. Hair and a modern wardrobe would be built in Blender or commissioned.
  • The default look is plain realism. It needs a stylized shader and better textures to not look like a 2010 tech demo.

Zero license risk, most Blender hours. Pick this if you'd rather spend time than money, or if you want the public repo.

Best faces per dollar, with a one-click process that converts hair to game cards, bakes textures, builds LODs and renames bones for engines. Lives in Blender, so it fits a GLB pipeline well.

  • Thin clothing library, so the wardrobe is still on us.
  • Its license bans sharing assets in easily extractable formats such as FBX, which a GLB in a browser arguably is. Needs a written waiver, same as path A.
  • The $68 personal tier excludes games, so it's the $128 tier or nothing.

A good middle path if the Reallusion fine print scares you off but you still want photoreal faces.

Fallback: Quaternius Universal Base Characters (free, CC0, about 13k tris, 20 hairstyles, humanoid rig, GLB native) still works as a prototype body to get the creator and netcode running this month while the real system is chosen. It fails the brief on faces: six fixed heads and no modern wardrobe.

Mixamo license, read fairly. The Mixamo terms themselves only ban AI training. Adobe's general terms let you embed the files in your product and distribute them only as part of it, never standalone. The anti-extraction rule exists for Adobe's sample files but not for Mixamo clips. Strict reading: a public GLB is extractable. Practical reading: clips baked into our character files are embedded in the product. My judgement is that it's realistically fine as long as the clips are never offered as a pack and never sit in a public repo.

Skip MoCap Online even though it has the best gun mocap. Its license requires binary-only distribution with files that can't be extracted, and buying it through Unity or Unreal doesn't change that.

ActorCore is the native match for Character Creator and the quality is excellent, but its license text bans embedding in online services, so it needs Reallusion's written OK. With Mixamo retargeting in one click on CC5, we can live without it.

Licensing and hosting, the one-paragraph version

Every paid character system and most paid animation packs require that players can't pull the raw files out. A browser game serves files to the browser, so plain GLBs break that rule. The fix is the same for all of them: pack meshes, textures and clips into a compressed, obfuscated bundle that the page decodes at load, keep the repo private, and host on Cloudflare Pages or Netlify. That's a normal game-dev practice and it's what Unity's own web builds do. Only path B, MPFB with CC0 content, avoids the whole question. This is my reading, not legal advice; for the Reallusion route a short email asking for written confirmation is worth sending before spending.

Decisions for you

Nothing gets bought, downloaded or built until you say so.

  1. Path: A (Character Creator 5, best result, about $600 to $900 all in), B (MPFB, free, more Blender), or C (Human Generator, $128, photoreal faces, thin wardrobe)?
  2. Creator style: OK with preset-based customization (pick a head, hair, outfit, colors) instead of face sliders? Path A requires it, and I'd recommend it for all paths.
  3. Mixamo archive: OK for me to download the full Mixamo library (about 2,450 clips, several GB) to your machine before the service degrades further? It needs your Adobe login in the browser.
  4. Hosting: private repo on Cloudflare Pages or Netlify, with packed asset bundles?
  5. Prototype now: start the creator and netcode on the free Quaternius body while the real system is chosen, or wait?
